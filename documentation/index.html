Stocky · version 5.8

Inventory, point of sale, online store & accounting

One Laravel application for the counter, the stockroom, the web shop and the books. A touch-first register that keeps selling offline, live stock across every warehouse, purchases and returns, double-entry accounting, HRM, sixty reports and a storefront with five themes. This guide covers installing it and running it.

In a hurry? Upload the files, open your domain, and follow the four-step installer. Then read Your first hour — the shortest path from an empty install to a shop that can scan a product, take cash and print a receipt.

Welcome

Stocky is one system for one business. The product the cashier scans is the same row the online shop sells, the row a WooCommerce or Shopify sync pushes, the row a purchase order restocks and the row the stock-aging report counts. Nothing is copied between screens, so nothing has to be reconciled — one catalogue, one stock figure, every channel.

What is in the box

Point of sale

Touch-first register with barcode and camera scanning, hold and drafts, split payments, promotions, registers, receipts, a customer display and an offline queue.

Products & stock

Single, variable, service and combo products; multi-warehouse stock with rack locations; batches and expiry; serial numbers; adjustments, transfers, counts and damages.

Buying & selling

Sales, quotations, purchases, sale and purchase returns, shipments, a customer ledger and supplier history; PDF, email, SMS and print on every document.

Online store

Five themes on one catalogue, twelve payment gateways, shipping zones, coupons, flash sales, reviews, wishlists, returns and a customer account.

Customer portal

A separate login for business customers: invoices, payments, statement, quotations, appointments, contracts and a knowledge base.

Money

Cash accounts, deposits and expenses, plus double-entry accounting that posts itself: chart of accounts, journals, trial balance, P&L, balance sheet and tax summary.

HRM

Companies, departments, shifts, employees, attendance, leave and payroll; recruitment and meetings as separate modules.

Business modules

Projects, bookings, service jobs, subscriptions, manufacturing, assets, fleet, kitchen display, documents, contracts, marketing, hospital and school — each on a switch.

Integrations

WooCommerce, Shopify, Salla, Jumia, PrestaShop, QuickBooks, Xero, Google Sheets, Mailchimp, Slack, Telegram, Google Calendar, ZATCA and Stripe.

Reports

Sixty report screens with a date range, a warehouse filter and Excel or PDF export, plus analytics, a 3D sales dashboard and question-driven AI reports.

Platform

Roles with 300+ permissions, users scoped to warehouses, 24 languages with RTL, light and dark themes, installable PWAs, a browser installer and an in-panel updater.

Mobile

A Bearer-token API for the separately sold companion app, with branding and module switches in Settings → Mobile App.

How to read this guide

Sections follow the order you will actually need them: install it, set the business up, then each module in the order the sidebar lists it. Every screen named here exists in the product — where something is deliberately absent it is listed under Deliberately not here rather than described as if it were built. Paths such as Settings → POS Settings refer to the sidebar; the address bar shows the same page under /next/….

The shape of every screen

Lists work the same way throughout: a search box and filters at the top of the card, a table, paging at the bottom, and export buttons where a list is worth exporting. Record pages open with a header — the reference, the status pills, the buttons that change its state — and then the detail. The POS is the exception: it is a full-screen application of its own, opened from the purple POS button in the header.

Server requirements

Stocky runs on ordinary PHP hosting. There is no Docker, no Node on the server and nothing to compile — the front-end assets ship already built. Cron and a queue worker are optional; see Cron & queue for what they add.

DatabaseMySQL 8.0+ or MariaDB 10.6+Create an empty database first; the installer fills it.
Extensionsopenssl, pdo, pdo_mysql, mbstring, curl, tokenizer, xml, ctype, fileinfo, gd, json, bcmath, zipAll standard. The installer checks each one and names the missing ones.
Web serverApache with mod_rewrite, or NginxPoint the domain at public/. The shipped .htaccess handles the rewrites.

Point the domain at public/, not at the folder above it. If the document root is the project folder, anyone can download your .env — which holds the database password and the application key. On cPanel this is Domains → Document Root. If your host truly will not allow it, the shipped .htaccess and server.php in the project root rewrite requests into public/; still prefer the proper document root.

Optional hardware

Nothing is bundled and nothing is proprietary. A USB or Bluetooth barcode scanner behaves as a keyboard and needs no driver. A phone or tablet camera scans in the browser. Thermal receipt printers print through the browser dialog, silently through QZ Tray on the cashier's computer, or directly to a network printer by IP address. Cash drawers kick through the receipt printer. Label printers take the barcode sheets. Digital scales that print weight-embedded barcodes are read as product plus kilograms.

Installing Online

Stocky installs itself in a browser. You do not need shell access, phpMyAdmin or a command line.

  1. Upload and unzip

    Put the contents of the ZIP on your server and point the domain at the public/ folder. Everything is included — you do not need to run composer install or npm.

  2. Open /setup

    Browse to https://your-domain/setup. You land on the requirements check. Every card must be green — the PHP version, the twelve extensions and the ZipArchive class. If one is not, enable it on the server and press Re-check requirements. Nothing has been written yet.

  3. Application settings

    The application name, the environment (Production for a live shop), debug mode (Off on a live server), an application key — press Generate — and the application URL, exactly as people will type it, with https://. All of this lands in .env and can be edited there later.

  4. Database

    Host, port, database name, username and password for an empty MySQL database. Press Test connection; the Continue button only appears once the test passes.

  5. Review and install

    Check the summary, then press Install Stocky. This writes .env, creates every table, installs the API keys and seeds the reference data: the Owner role, the permissions, the default warehouse, currencies, payment methods, settings and the translations for all 24 languages. Keep the tab open; it can take a few minutes on slow hosting.

  6. Sign in

    The finish page shows the default administrator: admin@example.com with the password 123456. Press Go to login.

Change the administrator password right after your first login. It is printed in this guide and on the installer's finish page. Open the user menu (top right) → Profile, or edit the account under User Management → Users — change the email as well.

The installer seals itself. Once it finishes, every /setup address answers 403. The seal is a file: storage/app/public/installed. Delete it to open the wizard again on a broken install — and if /setup is ever reachable when it should not be, that file is the first thing to check.

Installing Locally

To try Stocky on a Windows computer before you go live, install it on Laragon or XAMPP. The browser installer is the same one described above; only the preparation differs. Laragon is the easier of the two — it creates a local domain pointing at public/ on its own, and it ships MySQL 8. XAMPP needs a virtual host written by hand.

A local install is for trying things out, training staff and testing changes. An online store, payment gateways, marketplace webhooks and customer emails need a real server with a public https:// address.

Laragon

  1. Install Laragon and check PHP

    Download Laragon from laragon.org and install it in C:\laragon. Open Menu → PHP → Version: Stocky needs PHP 8.2 or newer. If only an older one is listed, download a PHP 8.2+ build (VS16 x64 Thread Safe ZIP) from windows.php.net, unzip it into its own folder under C:\laragon\bin\php\, and pick it in the same menu.

  2. Enable the extensions

    Menu → PHP → Extensions. Make sure curl, fileinfo, gd, mbstring, openssl, pdo_mysql and zip are ticked. Laragon restarts Apache on its own after each change.

  3. Copy the files

    Unzip Stocky into C:\laragon\www\stocky, so that C:\laragon\www\stocky\public exists — not C:\laragon\www\stocky\stocky\public. The folder name becomes the local address.

  4. Start and get the address

    Run Laragon as administrator the first time (it needs to write the Windows hosts file) and press Start All. With Menu → Preferences → General → Auto create virtual hosts ticked (the default), Laragon creates http://stocky.test and points it at the public/ folder. If the address does not open, press Reload in Laragon.

  5. Create the database

    Press Database in Laragon to open HeidiSQL and connect with user root and an empty password. Right-click the connection → Create new → Database, name it stocky, collation utf8mb4_unicode_ci.

  6. Run the installer

    Open http://stocky.test/setup and follow the four steps. Application URL: http://stocky.test. Database: host 127.0.0.1, port 3306, database stocky, user root, password empty.

XAMPP

  1. Install XAMPP

    Download the PHP 8.2 or newer build of XAMPP from apachefriends.org and install it in C:\xampp. Open the XAMPP Control Panel and start Apache and MySQL.

  2. Enable the extensions

    Control Panel → Apache Config → PHP (php.ini). Remove the leading ; from any of these lines that still have one, raise the two limits, save, then Stop and Start Apache:

    extension=curl
extension=fileinfo
extension=gd
extension=mbstring
extension=openssl
extension=pdo_mysql
extension=zip

max_execution_time=300
memory_limit=512M

  3. Copy the files

    Unzip Stocky into C:\xampp\htdocs\stocky, so that C:\xampp\htdocs\stocky\public exists.

  4. Add a virtual host

    XAMPP does not create local domains. Open C:\xampp\apache\conf\extra\httpd-vhosts.conf in a text editor and add at the end:

    <VirtualHost *:80>
    ServerName localhost
    DocumentRoot "C:/xampp/htdocs"
</VirtualHost>

<VirtualHost *:80>
    ServerName stocky.test
    DocumentRoot "C:/xampp/htdocs/stocky/public"
    <Directory "C:/xampp/htdocs/stocky/public">
        AllowOverride All
        Require all granted
    </Directory>
</VirtualHost>

    The first block keeps http://localhost and phpMyAdmin working. Then open Notepad as administrator, open C:\Windows\System32\drivers\etc\hosts, add the line below, save, and restart Apache:

    127.0.0.1    stocky.test

  5. Create the database

    Open http://localhost/phpmyadmin → Databases. Name stocky, collation utf8mb4_unicode_ci, Create.

  6. Run the installer

    Open http://stocky.test/setup and follow the four steps. Application URL: http://stocky.test. Database: host 127.0.0.1, port 3306, database stocky, user root, password empty.

Check XAMPP's database version. XAMPP bundles MariaDB, and recent builds still ship MariaDB 10.4 — older than the MariaDB 10.6 in the requirements. phpMyAdmin shows the version on its home page. If the install stops on a database error, use Laragon (MySQL 8) instead.

Use a domain, not a sub-folder. Open Stocky at http://stocky.test, not http://localhost/stocky. Stocky expects to sit at the root of its own address; under a sub-folder, links and the admin screens can break.

To move a local install to a live server later, export the database, upload the files and the public/images folder, import the database there, and change APP_URL and APP_DEBUG=false in .env.

Updating

Settings → Update. Three tabs: Automatic Update, Manual Update and the Update Guide. The installed version is shown against the latest one published on the update server.

Automatic update

Only available from version 5.8. The automatic updater ships with version 5.8. If you are on an older version, use the manual update to reach 5.8 first; after that, every later update can be installed from this page.

  1. Back up

    The updater takes its own backup of the files and the database, but take a database backup of your own first anyway.

  2. Upload the ZIP

    Download the new version from CodeCanyon and drop the full application ZIP onto the page. Nothing is touched yet: the pre-update checks validate the archive, its structure, the version, PHP requirements, permissions, disk space and the database connection.

  3. Install

    Press Install. Stocky backs up the files and the database to storage/app/updater/backups, puts the site into maintenance mode, replaces the files, runs the migrations and permission sync, and comes back. Keep the tab open; a bypass link is shown in case you lose the page.

  4. Reload

    Hard-refresh the browser (Ctrl + Shift + R) so it loads the new admin files. Check the version number in the footer.

If anything fails the previous version is restored automatically. If the rollback itself cannot finish, the page says Recovery required and offers Open Recovery Console — a standalone page at /system-update/recovery that works even in maintenance mode, where you can continue where it stopped, roll back, or discard the run. The pre-update backup stays intact until you resolve it. Update backups keeps the last few (three by default); the only remaining complete one can never be deleted.

Manual update

For hosts where the automatic updater cannot write, or if you prefer to see each step. The Manual Update tab describes it; in short:

  1. Back up the database and the public/images and storage/app folders.
  2. Upload the new files over the old ones. Do not delete or replace your .env file, the public/images/ folder or the storage/ folder — they hold your configuration, uploaded images and generated files, and removing them would break the installation and lose your data.
  3. Open https://your-domain/update.
  4. Hard-refresh the browser.

Custom work is overwritten by an update. If you or a developer have customized any files, those changes will be lost when you update.

Your first hour

A fresh install already knows the shape of a shop — it has a default warehouse, a walk-in customer, a set of payment methods and a currency. This is the shortest route from there to a counter that sells.

  1. Company details

    Settings → System Settings. Company name, logo, phone, email and address print on every receipt and invoice. Set the default currency, the timezone, the date and price formats, and the default warehouse and customer the POS opens with.

  2. Warehouses

    Settings → Warehouses. One is enough. Add one per shop, store-room or van if stock physically lives in different places — every stock figure is per warehouse, and users can be limited to the warehouses they may see.

  3. Categories, brands and units

    Products → Categories / Brand / Units. Short lists you edit rarely. Units can convert (a carton of 12 pieces); categories can carry an icon and an image for the POS tiles and the store.

  4. Products

    Products → Create product for a few, Import products for a spreadsheet. A product needs a name, a code, a category, a unit, a cost and a price; everything else — barcode symbology, tax, images, variants, stock alerts — can come later.

  5. Opening stock

    Products → Opening Stock imports a quantity per product per warehouse. Or receive your first purchase, which is the honest way to start a stock figure with a cost behind it.

  6. Payment methods

    Settings → Payment Methods. Cash, card, bank transfer and so on are seeded; hide the ones you do not take. Add a cash account under Accounting → List accounts if you want payments to land in named accounts.

  7. The receipt

    Settings → POS Receipt. Pick the paper width (58 or 80 mm), one of the five layouts, and what prints on it — the preview on the right is live. Settings → POS Settings holds the rest: auto-print, hold sales, the cash drawer and keyboard shortcuts.

  8. Users

    User Management → Group Permissions to create a Cashier role with only the POS and sales permissions, then Users to add each person with that role and the warehouses they may see.

  9. Open the POS and sell something

    Press the purple POS button in the header. Open the register with a float, pick the warehouse, tap or scan a product, Pay Now, cash, Complete Payment. The receipt prints; the stock moved.

Everything else — the online store, accounting, HRM, the marketplaces, the business modules — can wait. Turn off what you do not use in Settings → Modules so the sidebar shows only what your business runs.

Demo catalogues

Four seeders fill the store with a themed catalogue, complete with pictures, so you can see a theme with real products before entering your own. Each one switches the store to its theme and is safe to run more than once:

php artisan db:seed --class=ElectronicsDemoSeeder
php artisan db:seed --class=ToysDemoSeeder
php artisan db:seed --class=GroceryDemoSeeder
php artisan db:seed --class=RealEstateDemoSeeder

For a general set of customers, suppliers, products, sales, purchases, quotations and expenses without any theme, use the demo-data generator in Settings → System Settings — it works from the panel and needs no shell.

Demo data is a lot to delete afterwards. Seed it on a test install, not on the database you will trade with.

Business modules

Settings → Modules. Twenty-three optional modules, each with its own menu group, pages, permissions and reports. Switch off what your business does not do and the sidebar shrinks to match. Core commerce — products, sales, purchases, people, settings and reports — cannot be turned off.

A module that is off is also off for the router: its pages answer not found even by direct address, and its permissions disappear from the role editor. Turning it back on restores everything — no data is deleted by the switch.

Settings → Module Settings is a different page: it lists add-on modules installed as packages and offers Upload Module for a ZIP supplied by the vendor. A fresh install lists none.

Warehouses & locations

Settings → Warehouses. A warehouse is any place stock physically lives: a shop, a stockroom, a van, a market stall. Every stock quantity, every alert and every sale, purchase, transfer and adjustment is tied to one.

Internal locations

The Locations tab holds aisle, rack and bin positions inside a warehouse. Products can be assigned to a location so the picker knows where to walk; the Internal Location report lists what sits where.

Who sees which warehouse

Each user is assigned the warehouses they may work in (User Management → Users). The POS, the lists and the reports then only offer those. A cashier in the downtown shop never sees the airport store's stock; a manager assigned to all of them can switch. The default warehouse from System Settings is where the POS opens.

Users & roles

User Management → Users. A user is anyone who signs in to the admin or the POS. Each has a role, a status (inactive users cannot sign in but keep their history), an avatar and the warehouses they may see.

The install ships one role, Owner, which holds every permission — the administrator account has it. Create a role per job, not per person: Cashier, Stock keeper, Accountant, Store manager. Then give people the role.

Users can be signed out remotely from Settings → Login Devices, and every sign-in is listed in the Login Activity report.

Permissions

User Management → Group Permissions. A role is a named set of permissions. The editor lists more than 300 of them, grouped by module, with a search box and a select-all per group.

Most groups follow the same pattern — View, Create, Edit, Delete — with extras where a module has a sensitive action: override FEFO / sell quarantined under batches, write off, Pay due, report-level permissions, and the settings pages one by one, so a manager can be given POS Settings without Payment Gateway.

A cashier role

The minimum for somebody who only runs the till: POS, Sales → View and Create, Customers → View and Create (for quick-add at the counter), and nothing under Settings, Purchases or Reports. Add Sales → Edit only if they should be able to change a completed sale, and Cash Register Report if they close their own drawer.

Permissions apply to the API as well as the menu. A page hidden from the sidebar is also refused when typed into the address bar, and the mobile app sees the same set.

Payment methods & accounts

Settings → Payment Methods. The ways money arrives: the install seeds Cash, Credit Card, Check, TPE, Western Union, bank transfer and other. Rename, add or deactivate them; the POS payment screen and every payment form offer the active ones. Store credit and wallet are handled by the e-wallet module.

Accounts (Accounting → List accounts) are where the money is kept: the till, the bank account, the mobile-money float. Every payment, deposit and expense may name an account, and the account balance moves with it. The default account in System Settings is pre-selected on new payments. See Accounts, deposits & expenses.

Payment gateways are separate: Settings → Payment Gateway holds the Stripe keys the POS uses for card payments; the online store's twelve gateways are under Store → Payment Gateway.

Products

The product page

Every product has a detail page with its stock per warehouse, a movement history — every sale, purchase, return, transfer and adjustment that touched it, in order — and the sales and purchase lines it appeared on. The Product Report under Reports is the same view with a date range and export.

Variants, combos & services

The type at the top of the product form decides how stock behaves.

Single

One code, one price, one stock figure per warehouse. Most products.

Variable

A parent with variants — size, colour, capacity. Each variant has its own code, price, cost, image and stock. The POS and the store show the parent and let you pick the variant.

Service

No stock at all. Repairs, delivery, labour, consultations. Can carry a price and a tax like any product; the store can offer request a quote instead of a price.

Combo

A bundle sold as one line at one price. Selling it deducts each component's stock; the combo itself has none.

Subscription products (their own module, see Subscriptions) add a billing cycle to any product. Packs and wholesale tiers are pricing on top of a single or variable product, switched on under Feature switches.

Barcode labels

Products → Print Labels. Pick products (or a whole purchase), set a quantity per product, choose one of the five label templates and what prints on it — shop name, product name, price, barcode number — and print. The preview is true to size.

Labels go to a normal printer as a sheet, or directly — no print dialog — to a TSPL label printer over USB or the network. Products without a barcode of their own are printed with their product code, which the POS scanner reads just the same.

Batches & expiry

For food, pharmacy, cosmetics and anything with a shelf life. Turn batch tracking on per product; from then on every purchase line asks for a batch number and an expiry date, and stock is kept per batch.

Sales allocate first-expiry-first-out: the POS takes from the batch that expires soonest. Expired or quarantined batches are skipped unless the user holds the override FEFO / sell quarantined permission. Batches can be written off, and the Expiry report lists what expires in the coming days by warehouse; the Batch Register and Batch History reports show where each batch went.

Serial numbers

With the Track serial number / IMEI switch on (Feature switches) a product can require one serial per unit. Receiving a purchase asks for the serials; selling asks you to pick which units leave; returns bring a specific unit back. Phones, laptops, appliances, tools with a warranty.

Four reports cover them: Available Serial Numbers, Sold Serial Numbers, Serial Movement Log and Product Serial Inventory. The Warranty & Guarantee report combines the serial with the product's warranty period to show what is still covered.

The register

The purple POS button in the header opens the register full-screen. It is built for a touch screen and a scanner: large product tiles with image, code, stock and price; category and brand filters; a search box that also takes a scanner; and a cart on the left with a running total.

Opening the register

With registers enabled, the top-left pill reads CLOSED until you open one. Press it, enter the opening float, and the register is yours for the shift. Every sale, payment and hold is stamped with the register and the cashier. At the end of the shift press the pill again, enter the counted cash and a closing note, and the Cash Register report shows the float, the cash sales, the counted amount and the difference — per register and per cashier.

Warehouse and customer

The warehouse chip picks which stock you are selling from — the POS opens on the default warehouse from System Settings, limited to the warehouses your user may see. The customer chip defaults to walk-in-customer; search for a named customer to attach the sale to their ledger and their loyalty points, or press the person-plus icon to create one without leaving the screen.

Building the cart

  • Tap a tile or scan a barcode. A USB or Bluetooth scanner types into the search box; the scan icon opens the phone or tablet camera as a scanner (HTTPS required). A weight-embedded barcode from a scale adds the product with the weight as the quantity.
  • Variants ask which one; batch products allocate first-expiry-first-out; serial products ask you to pick the unit; packs ask single, 6-pack or case.
  • Each line has a quantity, a unit, a per-line discount, a tax and a price level — Retail or Wholesale. With wholesale tiers on, the price drops itself when the quantity reaches a tier and the line says so.
  • Below the lines: an order tax, an order discount, shipping, a promo code box and the promotions that apply.
  • Hold parks the sale under a reference so you can serve the next person and recall it; Recent Drafts lists what is parked. Drafts survive a page reload and, with offline mode, a lost connection.
  • A sale can also be saved as a quotation and turned into an invoice later.

Live sales counter

Sales → Real-time Sales Counter is a board for a back-office screen: the count and value of today's sales, refreshed by polling every few seconds with a chime when a new sale arrives. It needs no websocket server.

Taking payment

Pay Now opens the payment screen. The total is pre-filled; pick a method and press Complete Payment, or split it.

  • Split payments. Add Payment Method adds a second line — part cash, part card, part bank transfer, part wallet. The breakdown on the left shows what is paid, the balance and the change.
  • Quick cash. The buttons on the right enter the exact total or a round note; the change is computed.
  • Partial payment and credit. Pay less than the total and the sale is saved as partial with a due balance on the customer's ledger. A customer with a credit limit cannot go past it. Pay due on the sales list settles it later.
  • Store credit and wallet. With the e-wallet module, a customer's balance is a payment method.
  • Loyalty points. A named customer's available points show on the screen; convert them into a discount, or redeem a reward from the loyalty catalogue. Points earned by the sale are credited when it completes.
  • Card through Stripe. With keys in Settings → Payment Gateway, the card method charges through Stripe from the register; refunds sync back.
  • Kitchen routing. With the kitchen display on, the sale can be sent to the kitchen, saved without sending, or sent later.

Completing the payment saves the sale, moves the stock, posts the journal (if auto journals are on), credits the loyalty points, prints the receipt (if auto-print is on) and, if a cash drawer is configured, kicks it open.

Offline mode

The register keeps selling when the internet does not. Switch it on with offline sync in Settings → System Settings → System.

  1. The register caches

    While online, the POS keeps a copy of the catalogue and the customers in the browser, and refreshes it as you work. The status pill bottom left reads Online · synced.

  2. The connection drops

    The pill turns red and reads Offline. Tiles, search, the cart, holds and drafts keep working from the cache. Sales you complete are queued in the browser with their payments, and the pill shows how many are waiting. Reloading the page while offline is fine — the register comes back from the cache.

  3. It comes back

    The queue uploads itself, oldest first; each sale gets its reference and moves the stock as if it had been taken online. The pill returns to Online · synced. If a queued sale is refused — a product deleted meanwhile, say — it stays in the queue with the error so nothing is silently lost.

What offline cannot do. Opening or closing the register, card payments through Stripe, kitchen tickets, customer-display updates and anything that needs the server to answer. Stock figures on the tiles are as of the last sync, so two offline tills can both sell the last unit. The queue lives in that browser's storage — do not clear site data while sales are waiting.

Customer display

A second screen facing the customer — a tablet on a stand, a monitor, an old phone — that mirrors the cart as the cashier works: the lines, the totals, and a thank-you when the sale completes. It is a web page, so any device with a browser on the same network will do.

  1. Generate a token

    Sales → Customer Screen → Generate New Token. The page shows the display URL — /customer-display?token=… — and a QR code for it.

  2. Open it on the display device

    Scan the QR or type the URL. The page installs as a PWA and runs full-screen. It supports light and dark themes and needs no login — the token is the credential.

  3. Sell

    The display updates as items are added. Tokens expire after 24 hours; generate a new one to rotate access, and the old link stops working at once.

If the display does not connect, check that the device is on the same network and uses the full URL including the token. If items stop updating, make sure the POS is still open on the cashier's side — the display shows the last cart the register broadcast.

Kitchen display

For restaurants, cafés and anywhere an order is prepared after it is taken. Switch it on under Feature switches; the POS then offers Send to Kitchen on every sale and Kitchen → Kitchen Display appears in the sidebar.

  • Tickets carry a daily token number, the items with special instructions, the customer and the source (POS or online). Start preparation, check items off one by one — the ticket completes itself when the last one is done — Mark completed, with a ten-second Undo. A ticket whose sale was deleted shows Voided.
  • Overdue colours. Set a preparation-time target in Features; tickets past half of it turn amber, past all of it turn red, pulse and jump to the top of their lane.
  • Stations. Define stations by product category — grill, bar, dessert — and pin a screen to one; it shows only its items. Items in a category no station claims appear on every station, so nothing is missed. The All stations view is the expo screen with full tickets.
  • Kitchen mode is full-screen for a wall-mounted TV; chits print at 72 mm, station-aware.
  • Online orders can create a ticket automatically when confirmed.

The order-ready screen

A customer-facing board of token numbers under Preparing and Ready, with a chime when one moves. Press Order Ready Screen on the kitchen display to generate a secure link (/order-ready?token=…), open it on a screen in the collection area, and it updates on its own. The link stays valid for seven days; generating a new one replaces it.

Sales

Sales → All Sales. Every invoice, from the POS or from the back office, in one list: date, reference, customer, warehouse, status, grand total, paid, due, payment status and shipping status. Filter by any of them, export, and act from the row — Pay due, print, PDF, email, SMS, edit, return, delete.

Creating a sale in the back office

The form is the POS in a page: customer, warehouse, date, lines with discount, tax, unit and batch or serial, an order tax, discount and shipping, a status (pending, ordered, complete), a sales agent, a note, and a payment you can record at once. Stock moves when the sale is completed. With multi-currency on, pick the sale currency and the base-currency equivalent shows alongside.

The sale page

Payments are recorded against the sale with a date, a method and an account; each one updates the paid and due figures and the customer's ledger. Attach a file — a signed delivery note, a purchase order from the customer — under documents. Email and SMS use the templates from Settings, merged with the sale.

Quotations

Quotations. A priced offer that moves no stock. Same form as a sale; same lines; a status of pending or sent. Print or PDF it, email it, and when the customer agrees press Create sale — the quotation's lines become a sale in one click, and the quotation is marked as converted.

Business customers can also see their quotations in the client portal, accept them there, or request a new one; shoppers can send a quote request from the store for service products.

Returns

A return is always against an original document, so the stock, the money and the reports agree on what came back.

  • Sale return — from the sale's row or page. Pick the lines and quantities coming back (a serial product asks which unit); stock returns to the warehouse; the refund is recorded as a payment on the return, by any method, or left as a credit on the customer's ledger.
  • Purchase return — the mirror image, against a purchase, with the supplier's refund recorded the same way.
  • Store returns — shoppers request a return from their account within the return window; you approve it under Store → Returns Requests and it becomes a sale return.

The Return Ratio report shows what comes back most, by product and by customer.

Shipments

Sales → Shipments. One shipment per sale that needs delivering: the carrier, a tracking number, the delivery address and a status — packed, shipped, delivered, cancelled. The shipping status shows on the sale and on the sales list, and the store's customer account shows it to the shopper.

To hand the parcel to a courier company, print its label and have the status follow by itself, use Couriers; those shipments are listed here as well.

Suppliers & purchases

People → Suppliers holds who you buy from — name, contact, tax number, address, opening balance — with a detail page listing every purchase, return and payment. Purchases is how stock arrives with a cost behind it.

Receiving a purchase

Supplier, warehouse, date, lines with the unit cost, the purchase unit, a discount and a tax; per line a batch number and expiry for batch products and the serials for serial products; an order tax, discount and shipping. The status is pending, ordered or received — stock moves when it is received. Record the supplier payment at once or later from the row. A purchase can also open the label printer for everything it contains.

The product's cost is what you paid; Inventory Valuation uses it, and the profit reports subtract it from the sale price. Purchases can be imported from CSV to bring in history; see Importing.

Couriers: how it works

Couriers in the sidebar. Send a sale to a courier company in one click, print its label, and let the courier's own updates — picked up, out for delivery, delivered, returned, cash collected and paid out — arrive in Stocky by themselves. Forty couriers are built in, from Pakistan, the Gulf, Egypt, Bangladesh, India and Southeast Asia to DHL, FedEx and UPS, plus shipping aggregators that reach hundreds more and a manual courier for anyone without an API.

Book

From a sale, the sales list (one or many at once) or the sale page. The form fills itself from the order: receiver, address, COD amount, weight. Aggregators and global couriers show live prices to pick from.

Track

Couriers that can push send each change to Stocky the moment it happens; the rest are checked on a schedule you choose. Every update lands on the shipment's timeline and the sale's shipping status.

Settle

Delivered COD parcels are marked collected; when the courier pays you out, the payment is recorded on the sale. Courier charges can be saved as expenses, and returned parcels go to a review queue or straight back into stock.

Keep customers posted

SMS, email or WhatsApp messages at the statuses you pick, and a public tracking page in your online store where customers follow their parcel.

Measure

A courier report with delivery and return rates, COD and charges by courier and by city, and a return-risk badge on customers who often send parcels back.

Any courier

No API? Add it as Manual / Other courier and type the tracking number, or use AfterShip or 17TRACK so the status follows by itself for thousands of carriers.

The sidebar group has six pages: Courier Shipments, Courier report, Courier Accounts, Courier Settings, Courier logs and Courier guide. The sale page gains a Courier shipment panel, and the sales list a Send to courier action.

Courier guide is the in-app companion to this chapter: a checklist that shows how far your setup has gone, with a link for each step, a picker that shows any courier's keys, options, how its updates arrive and what to watch for, with an Add this courier button, and your accounts' webhook addresses with copy buttons.

Switching it on and who can use it

Courier accounts

Couriers → Courier Accounts lists your accounts with their connection state — Connected, Failed or Not tested — the warehouses they serve and which one is the default. You can hold several accounts for the same courier, for example one per warehouse or one per city.

  1. Choose the courier

    Add courier account, then pick the courier from the catalogue. Each card shows the courier's region and what it does — booking, labels, live rates, cancellation, status pushes, COD, city lists, pickup requests, test mode.

  2. Paste the API credentials

    Each courier asks for its own keys; the help under every field says where to find them, and Courier-by-courier setup sums them up. Saved secrets are never shown again: leave a field blank to keep the stored value.

  3. Test mode or live

    Couriers that offer a sandbox have a Mode switch. In Test the account talks to the courier's test system — bookings are not real and nobody comes to collect them. Sandbox and live keys are usually different, so paste the matching pair.

  4. Courier options and pickup address

    Courier options are that courier's own settings — a pickup location code, a service, a country. The Pickup address is where parcels are collected; couriers that work with full postal addresses (aggregators, DHL, FedEx, UPS, Ninja Van, Flash Express) also ask for the pickup postal code, state and two-letter country code, and a default parcel size.

  5. Booking defaults

    The warehouses the account serves (orders from those warehouses pick it automatically), a default weight in kg, a service type, and whether customers may open the parcel before paying. Tick Default account for the one used when no warehouse rule applies.

  6. Test the connection

    Save, then Test connection. Many couriers answer with something useful — your pickup addresses, stores, warehouses or package codes with the codes to paste into Courier options.

  7. Register the webhook URL

    Couriers that push status changes show a Webhook URL on the account once it is saved. Paste it in the courier's dashboard — the setup tables say where — so updates arrive instantly. Bosta, Sendbox and UPS Track Alert register it themselves with every parcel. Couriers that sign their updates — AfterShip, EasyPost, Shiprocket, OTO, Biteship, Pathao and Steadfast — also need the webhook secret you set there pasted on the account: until it is, their updates are rejected (Courier logs shows why).

The webhook URL is a secret. It identifies the account, and some couriers send their updates without a signature. Paste it only into that courier's dashboard. It is built from your site's address; if you move Stocky to another domain, update it at the courier.

Manual / Other courier has one extra field, Tracking link: the courier's public tracking page with {tracking} where the number goes, for example https://courier.example/track?id={tracking}. Staff and customers get a working link from it.

Sending orders to a courier

Three ways in: Send to courier in a sale's row menu on the sales list, the button on the sale page's Courier shipment panel, or — for many orders at once — tick sales on the list and press Send to courier (n) in the toolbar.

The booking form

Everything is filled from the sale and can be changed before booking:

  • Courier account — the warehouse's account, else the default one. Couriers like Manual, AfterShip and 17TRACK ask for the tracking number printed on the courier's receipt.
  • Receiver — name, phone, email and address from the online order's shipping details, or from the customer. Couriers with full postal addresses add state, postal code and country.
  • Courier city — couriers that work from their own city, area or zone list show a searchable picker. Your choice is remembered, so the next order to the same city picks it by itself.
  • Drop-off latitude and longitude — only for couriers that route by map point (Lalamove). Right-click the address in Google Maps to copy them.
  • Parcel — COD amount (the amount still due on the sale; 0 means prepaid), weight (from the products' weights, else the account default), pieces, contents and delivery instructions.

The customer's return-risk badge sits next to Receiver; a high-risk customer also gets a red warning above it.

Compare rates

Aggregators (EasyPost, Shippo, Shiprocket, Sendcloud, OTO, Torod, EasyParcel, Biteship, Shiplogic, Sendbox), DHL Express, FedEx, UPS and Lalamove offer Compare rates: prices and delivery days straight from the carriers for this exact parcel. Pick one and Book buys that option. Without a pick, the account's default service is used — or, for DHL, FedEx and UPS with no default, the cheapest quote.

Booking many orders

The bulk window lists every ticked sale as Ready, Incomplete (hover it to see what is missing — a phone, an address, a city) or Already booked. Choose the account, optionally one Weight for all (kg) instead of From products, and press Book n orders. Each order is booked on its own, so one rejection does not stop the rest; the result shows what was booked and why anything failed, with Print labels for the lot.

Labels

Print label prints the courier's own label or airway bill when the courier issues one — a PDF, a link, or for UPS a label image laid out for 4 × 6 paper. Couriers without labels get a Stocky label: 100 × 150 mm with the tracking barcode, sender, receiver and the amount to collect. Allow pop-ups for the Stocky, or the browser blocks the print window.

DHL Express, FedEx and UPS hand the label over only once, when the shipment is created; Stocky keeps it on the shipment, so reprinting is always possible.

Cancelling

Cancel booking works while the shipment is still Booked. Couriers with a cancel call (or UPS's void) are told; for the others, cancel in the courier's portal as well. Once the courier has the parcel it can only come back as a return. A cancelled sale can be sent again, and so can a sale whose parcel came back — the returned parcel keeps its own record and leaves the returns queue as Sent again. Once returned goods were restocked with a sale return, they are back in stock: ship them on a new sale.

Tracking shipments

Couriers → Courier Shipments. Every courier shipment with its tracking number, sale, customer, city, status, COD and return state. The tabs — All, On the way, Returns to review, COD to be paid out and Status check errors — have a counter card each on top; click a card to open its tab. Search by tracking number, sale or customer; filter by account, status or booking dates; tick rows to print their labels together.

The row menu holds the actions: Timeline, Check status now, Open tracking page, Print label, Update status, Update COD, the return actions, Cancel booking and View sale. The same shipment, with its timeline, shows on the sale page.

Courier logs

Couriers → Courier logs records every call Stocky makes to a courier — connection tests, bookings, rates, city lists, labels, cancellations, status checks — every update a courier sends, accepted or rejected, and every error. Filter by account, action, result, direction and date, or search inside the content; Details shows what was sent and the courier's exact answer, with a link to the shipment and the sale. It is the first place to look when a test fails, a booking is refused or statuses stop moving.

Keys, tokens and passwords are masked before anything is stored, and long content is shortened. Scheduled status checks are logged only when they fail; Check status now is always logged. Entries are kept 30 days; Clear logs older than 7 days and Delete all logs tidy up sooner. The page needs the Manage courier accounts and settings permission.

COD, charges & returns

Couriers → Courier Settings decides what Stocky does with money and returned parcels. Everything is optional and happens once per shipment, however many times the courier reports the same thing.

Cash on delivery

A COD shipment goes Not collected → Collected by courier (on delivery) → Paid out to you. Couriers that report payouts — PostEx, Leopards, TCS, Trax, M&P, Shiprocket, Pathao, RedX and eCourier — move the last step themselves, with the payout reference; for the others use Update COD.

With Record the payment on the sale when the courier pays out the COD amount on and an Account chosen, a payout is saved as a payment on the sale, into that account, with the payment method you pick — the sale's balance and the account balance follow. Without an account, payouts are only marked.

Courier charges

Save the delivery charges reported by the courier as an expense adds one expense in the chosen category when a shipment is delivered, returned or cancelled, for the charges the courier reported.

Returned parcels

  • Send to the review queue (recommended) — a returned parcel waits under Returns to review. Open it, look at the goods, then Restock (a sale return for the whole order, items back in stock) or Dismiss (no restock) for damaged or lost parcels.
  • Restock automatically — the sale return is created and the stock put back as soon as the courier reports the return.

A sale that already has a return is never restocked twice; the queue says why.

Customer messages & tracking page

Customer notifications

Courier Settings → Customer notifications. Switch on any of the three channels — they use the senders you already set up:

  • SMS through the gateway in SMS Settings.
  • Email through your mail server.
  • WhatsApp through WhatsApp Business: the same text as the SMS, sent through the Cloud API and kept in the WhatsApp message log. Meta delivers free-form text only within 24 hours of the customer's last message to you; outside that window the log shows Meta's error.

Then tick the statuses worth a message — shipped (booked), picked up, in transit, out for delivery, delivery attempt failed, delivered, returning. Each ticked status has its own text, filled with a sensible default you can edit or Reset to default. Placeholders: {contact_name}, {business_name}, {invoice_number}, {courier}, {tracking_number}, {tracking_url}, {cod_amount} and {cod_note} (“Please keep 1,500.00 ready.” on COD parcels, nothing on prepaid ones).

Messages go to the phone and email on the booking, else the customer's. Each status is sent at most once per parcel, and the timeline records every send — or why one failed — as Notification. A failing channel does not stop the others.

The tracking page

With Show the tracking page on, your online store answers at /track (under the store's address, e.g. https://yourshop.com/online_store/track). Customers enter the tracking number, or their order number with the phone number used on the order, and see the status, a progress bar, the courier and its scan history. The customer's first name, destination city and the courier's delivery details appear only after the order number and phone check — never for a bare tracking number. The page never shows the address or staff notes, and limits how often it can be queried.

{tracking_url} in messages points to this page. If the online store is off, it points to the courier's own tracking page instead.

Loyalty & promotions

Loyalty points

Promotions

Promotions. Rules that apply themselves at the POS checkout: a percentage or a fixed amount off, on chosen products, categories or the whole cart, within a date range, optionally behind a promo code, with a usage limit. E-wallet

Setting up the store

The online store sells the products you mark visible, from the warehouses you choose, with the same prices, stock and customers as the counter. It is switched on per install under Settings → Modules → Store, then configured from Store → Settings.

  1. Basics

    Store name, the theme, the warehouses it sells from (or all), the default language and the display currency. Online store enabled is the master switch; off, the storefront answers with a closed page.

  2. Address

    By default the store lives at /online_store. Set a custom URL path such as /shop, or tick use the root domain so the shop is your home page and the admin stays at /login and /next. On the root domain the shop's sign-in and register pages move under /customer/…. Old links are redirected.

  3. Stock behaviour

    Show stock or not, hide out-of-stock products, allow or prevent overselling, hide prices for guests (a wholesale catalogue that shows prices only after sign-in).

    Public registration on or off; require an invite code, admin approval, email verification, or any combination. See Customer accounts.

  5. Returns and reviews

    The return window in days, whether shoppers may cancel unshipped orders, and whether reviews publish at once or wait for moderation.

  6. Make products visible

    A product appears in the store only when its online store switch is on. Set it per product, or in bulk from the product list.

Accounting

Accounting → Dashboard, Chart of Accounts, Journal Entries and the financial reports. Proper double-entry books that post themselves: with the auto journal entries switch on (Feature switches), every sale, purchase, return, payment, expense, deposit and money transfer writes a balanced journal against the chart of accounts. Nothing has to be keyed twice.

Chart of accounts

A standard chart is created for you — cash and bank, receivables, inventory, payables, sales, cost of goods sold, expense accounts, tax accounts. Rename, renumber or add accounts to match your accountant's chart; the automatic journals map onto the accounts you mark for each purpose.

Journal entries

Automatic journals are posted as the document is saved and carry its reference. Manual journals — an accrual, a depreciation charge, an owner's drawing — are entered as draft, must balance, and are posted when you are sure. Posted journals are not edited; reverse them with a new entry.

The statements

Trial balance, profit and loss, balance sheet and tax summary, each for any period, each exportable to Excel and PDF. If you began trading before turning the switch on, the journals for existing documents are not created retroactively — post an opening-balance journal as of the start date instead. QuickBooks and Xero can take the invoices and payments if your accountant works there.

Multi-currency

Off by default. Turn it on under Feature switches, then give each currency in Settings → Currencies an exchange rate against the base currency. From then on a sale, a purchase or an online order can be recorded in another currency: the document keeps its own currency, rate and totals, and the base-currency equivalent is shown alongside and used everywhere else.

Rates are manual — you type them and update them; nothing is fetched. Reports, stock valuation, the ledger and the accounting journals stay in the base currency, so a euro sale and a dollar sale add up in one figure. The store's currency switcher is a separate, display-only feature that works even with this switch off.

WooCommerce

Ecommerce Platforms → WooCommerce. Two-way sync between Stocky and a WordPress shop: products, stock, categories, brands, customers and orders. Stocky is the source of truth for stock and catalogue; WooCommerce is where online orders are placed.

Connect the shop

  1. Create REST API keys in WooCommerce

    In WordPress: WooCommerce → Settings → Advanced → REST API → Add key, permission Read/Write. Copy the consumer key (ck_…) and consumer secret (cs_…) — the secret is shown only once.

  2. Optional: a WordPress application password

    Only needed for product images: the WooCommerce API moves everything else, while the WordPress API searches the Media Library and uploads images that are not there yet. Use a WordPress user who can manage media and create the password under Users → Profile → Application Passwords. Leave both fields empty and everything still syncs except images.

  3. Enter them in Stocky

    On the Settings tab: the store URL — the site address with https:// and no trailing slash, e.g. https://yoursite.com — the consumer key and secret, and optionally the WordPress username and application password. Save runs a connection test against the shop; the status tile turns Connected.

The first sync

Each tab — Products, Stock, Categories, Brands, Customers, Orders — shows what is linked and what is not, and offers a push (Stocky → WooCommerce) or a pull (WooCommerce → Stocky). On a fresh connection, go in this order:

  1. Categories, then brands

    So a pushed product lands in the right category.

  2. Products

    Push to publish your catalogue, or pull if the shop already has it. If both sides already have the same products, use Auto-link by SKU on the Products tab first, so nothing is created twice.

  3. Stock

    Push. From then on keep it right with the instant push and the hourly sync below.

  4. Customers and orders

    Pull. Orders become sales in Stocky.

Right away: push stock and products as they change

Off until you ask for it — and the setting people miss. Without it a POS sale does not reach WooCommerce when it happens: the shop finds out at the next hourly stock sync, and until then it can sell a unit you have already sold at the till.

  • Push stock the moment it changes (Settings tab): every stock movement — POS sale, return, adjustment, transfer, received purchase — sends a push of just those products. Only products already linked to WooCommerce are pushed, variable products go through their parent, and a combo is re-pushed when any of its components move. A twenty-line sale costs one push, not twenty.
  • Push products the moment they are saved: a product created, edited or duplicated is sent right after it is saved — details, price, variants and images, then its stock. Bulk imports are not pushed one by one; run a products sync after an import.

Neither needs a queue worker or a cron entry: the push runs after the screen has been answered, pushes never overlap, and if the shop stops answering they pause for five minutes (recorded in Logs) and catch up with the next push. Bulk operations such as deleting a product move stock without firing an event — keep the hourly stock sync on as the safety net.

On a schedule

WooCommerce has no webhook receiver here, so everything coming in — orders, product changes made in the shop, customers — arrives on the schedule. The five WooCommerce cron lines cover it; each runs on its own, so add only what you want. woocommerce:sync works through its own queue while it runs, so no separate worker is needed. Without any cron, every tab still syncs on demand while its page is open — orders then arrive only when someone presses the button.

The Settings tab has a Sync tuning card for slow or busy hosts: products per batch (default 25), stock products per batch (25), the HTTP timeout (30 s), retries on timeouts and 429/5xx answers (2), how long a run may sit before it counts as stuck (10 min), and media timeouts for image uploads. Stores → Add store. Every setting is changed the same way later — the pencil on the row, or Edit store on the store's page.

    A name, the shop domain (acme.myshopify.com; pasting the admin URL works too), the credential type and its values, the API version (default 2024-10) and, for a legacy app, the webhook signing secret.   2. Where data lands

    The Stocky warehouse and the Shopify location it maps to (Fetch locations from Shopify fills the list) — each store pairs one warehouse with one location, and inventory sync and order import refuse to run without them.   3. What to sync

    A switch per entity. Off here means off everywhere — manual, scheduled and webhooks.

Customers match on email; customers without one are skipped. Mappings lets you link anything by hand; unlinking only forgets the pair and deletes nothing.

Automatic sync

Two mechanisms keep a shop in step, and they cover different things — use both.

WebhooksScheduled sync
DirectionShopify → StockyBoth ways

NeedsA public HTTPS address and the signing secretThe shopify:sync cron line

  1. Subscribe to the webhooks

    On the store's page, Webhooks tab, press Register in Shopify. It subscribes the shop to every topic whose entity is switched on — orders/create, orders/updated, orders/cancelled, orders/fulfilled, products/create, products/update, customers/create, customers/update, inventory_levels/update — pointing at https://your-domain/api/shopify/webhook. Pressing it twice is safe; Tidy up removes stale subscriptions. Each delivery is recorded once, so Shopify's retries cannot create a second order, and one that failed can be Replayed from the same tab.

  2. Choose what runs on a schedule

    Edit the store, Automatic sync tab: switch it on, set a default interval (60 minutes unless changed) and build the plan. A step is one entity in one direction with its own interval — Orders · Shopify → Stocky every 15 min, Inventory · Stocky → Shopify every 30 min. Leave the plan empty and the store pulls orders and pushes stock at the default interval, which suits most shops. Add the shopify:sync cron line; five minutes is the finest interval it can honour.

  3. Selling the same stock at a counter? Push stock the instant it changes

    Off until you ask for it. On the same tab, Push stock the moment it changes makes any stock movement in the store's own warehouse — POS sale, return, adjustment, received purchase — push just those products within seconds. It needs Inventory switched on, the store connected, and a warehouse and location set; only mapped products are pushed. Nothing else to install: the push runs after the sale has been answered. Keep the scheduled inventory push as the safety net for bulk operations.

  4. Watch it

    The store's Overview shows every step with its last run, next due time and state — Waiting, Due now, Running, or Blocked with the reason (usually no warehouse or no location). Runs and Errors keep the history; scheduled runs appear exactly like manual ones.

  5. Large syncs run in the background

    A sync started from the Sync Centre answers at once and is worked by the Shopify queue cron line in short slices, each carrying on from where the last stopped — so hosting that ends long web requests (LiteSpeed, nginx, a load balancer) cannot cut a big catalogue off part-way, and closing the page does not stop it. Without that cron line the open Sync Centre page carries the run on itself after a few seconds, one short request at a time.

A step is due when it last started longer ago than its interval, so a sync you start by hand postpones the next automatic one. Do the first catalogue push by hand — after a --dry-run — before putting it on a schedule.

PrestaShop

  1. Create a Webservice key

    In the PrestaShop back office: Advanced Parameters → Webservice, enable the webservice, and add a key with View, Add, Edit on products and stock_availables, and View on orders and customers.

  "id": "4f0c7c9e-…",            // unique per delivery
  "event": "sale.created",
  "created_at": "2026-10-03T14:05:22+00:00",
  "data": {
    "id": 1042,
    "type": "Sale",
    "attributes": { "Ref": "SL_1042", "GrandTotal": 125.5, "statut": "completed", … }
  }
}

…and these headers: X-Webhook-Event, X-Webhook-Id (the webhook), X-Webhook-Delivery (this delivery), X-Webhook-Attempt and X-Webhook-Signature — sha256= followed by the hex HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body, keyed with the webhook's secret. Verify it before trusting the payload:

$body      = file_get_contents('php://input');
$expected  = 'sha256=' . hash_hmac('sha256', $body, 'whsec_your_secret');
$signature = $_SERVER['HTTP_X_WEBHOOK_SIGNATURE'] ?? '';

if (! hash_equals($expected, $signature)) {
    http_response_code(401);
    exit;
}

Answer with any 2xx status. Anything else — or no answer within the timeout — is retried after 1 minute, 5 minutes, 30 minutes and 2 hours; after the fifth attempt the delivery is marked failed. The Delivery Logs tab shows each one as pending, retrying, success or failed, with the payload, the response code and body, and the time taken. Use X-Webhook-Delivery to ignore a delivery you have already processed.

Google Calendar & payment gateways

  • Google Calendar (Settings → System Settings → Calendar) mirrors bookings into a calendar: a confirmed booking becomes an event, and changes and deletions follow. Create an OAuth client in Google Cloud with the Google Calendar API enabled and the redirect URI https://your-domain/google-calendar/callback, enter the client ID, secret and calendar ID (primary for your main calendar), then Connect. Mobile app & API

Stocky's admin, POS, store, customer display and portal are all installable web apps (PWAs). A native companion app is sold separately; this package holds its server side.

Settings → Mobile App: enable or disable sign-in from the app (with a maintenance message when it is off), the app name, logo, primary colour and theme mode, which modules it shows, whether the scanner, offline data and price editing are allowed, the minimum app version that may connect, and support contacts.

The API

The app authenticates at POST /api/mobile/login with an email and password and receives a Laravel Passport personal access token, sent on every later call as Authorization: Bearer …. GET /api/mobile/ping lets a client validate a server URL before signing in. The token carries the user's role, so the app sees exactly what the browser would.

The rest of the API — the endpoints the admin itself uses under /api/… — accepts the same Bearer token, which is how your own integrations read products, create sales or pull reports. Tokens issued to devices are listed, and can be revoked, under Settings → Login Devices.

Every endpoint — path, parameters, validation rules, required permission, response shapes and cURL / JavaScript / PHP samples — is documented in the API reference, which ships with this package. It also exposes an OpenAPI 3 document you can import into Postman or Insomnia, and a try-it console that sends real requests against your own server.

Backups

Settings → Backup. Generate backup dumps the database to storage/app/public/backup/; the list shows every dump with its date and size, to download or delete. With the cron entry in place the database:backup command runs on the schedule as well.

Cloud backup copies each dump off the server: Amazon S3 (or any S3-compatible bucket — key, secret, region, bucket, endpoint, path) or Google Drive (client ID and secret, refresh token, folder ID). A backup that lives only on the server it protects is not a backup.

The dump is the database only. Product images, attachments and documents live in public/images/ and storage/app/ — include those folders in your file backup. To restore, import the SQL into an empty database with phpMyAdmin or mysql, and copy the folders back. There is no restore button in the panel, on purpose: restoring overwrites everything.

System health & security

System Health: PHP and Laravel versions, environment, database size, storage usage, the queue status (pending jobs — a growing number means the scheduler is not running), the last backup date, and a PDF of it all for a support ticket.

Cron & queue

Stocky works without either — sales, stock, receipts, the store and the reports need nothing in the background. A single cron entry unlocks the scheduled work:

* * * * * cd /path/to/stocky && php artisan schedule:run >> /dev/null 2>&1

On cPanel this is Cron Jobs; use the full path to PHP 8.2+ if php is older. If you would rather schedule only the integrations you use, each has a cron line of its own — see Cron lines at a glance; use one style or the other, not both. What the scheduler runs:

Because the scheduler drains the queue each minute, you do not need a separate queue:work process; if you run one anyway (Supervisor, a systemd unit), the scheduler's pass simply finds nothing to do. Without any cron, queued jobs drain inline while somebody uses the admin, which is enough for a small shop but delays webhooks and syncs until the next visit.

Deliberately not here

Every link in the sidebar opens a real screen. A few things a system like this could plausibly do are absent on purpose, and this guide would rather name them than let you find out:

  • No native mobile app in this package. The admin, POS, store, customer display and portal are installable PWAs. Settings → Mobile App holds the server side of the separately sold companion app.
  • No AI model calls. AI Reports answers a fixed list of business questions from your own data. Nothing is sent to a third-party model, and no key is needed.
  • No multi-tenant or SaaS mode. One install is one business, with as many warehouses, users and stores as you like.
  • No tax engine. Tax rates are what you enter, per product and per store rate. ZATCA covers Saudi e-invoicing only.
  • No payroll tax tables. Payroll records what you pay; the deductions are yours to compute.
  • No live exchange rates. Multi-currency rates are typed and updated by you.
  • No hardware is bundled. Scanners, scales, printers and cash drawers are standard devices talking over the browser, QZ Tray or the network.
  • No restore button. Restoring a backup overwrites everything, and a mis-click there costs a business its history. Restore with phpMyAdmin or the command line, deliberately.
